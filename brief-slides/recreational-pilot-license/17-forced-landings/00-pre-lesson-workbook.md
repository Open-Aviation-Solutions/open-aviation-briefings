# 1. Why engines fail

Complete engine failure is rare in a well-maintained training aeroplane — but partial power loss (rough running, reduced power) is more common, and this lesson trains you to handle both. Knowing the common causes helps you recognise *which* kind of failure you're dealing with.

# 3. Choosing your field

With the engine gone, your gliding distance is fixed by height — so the field you choose has to be within easy reach, and good enough to land on safely. This is a skill you can start building now, from the ground, every time you fly as a passenger or drive through the countryside.

# Bring this to your lesson

- Bring your completed answers (printed, or your notebook) to the pre-flight brief
- We'll check through them together and answer anything that didn't make sense
- From there, the live theory brief builds on this: the immediate/vital actions sequence, applying your field-selection knowledge to a real scenario, flying the approach and correcting if you're too high or low, and recognising an over- or undershoot before it's too late

> None of this is about memorising trivia for its own sake — it's about building a plan so familiar that, if you're ever faced with a real engine failure, no time is wasted deciding what to do next.
