# Using these notes

The theory session is done and the student knows today involves simulated engine failures close to the ground — that can raise nerves, so keep it interactive. About **15–20 minutes** at the whiteboard: **ask, draw, and listen** rather than present. The two safety-critical pieces — the **pre-take-off safety brief** and the **engine failure after take-off actions** — are the heart of this briefing; the student should be able to say both back to you before you walk to the aircraft.

**The components** (choose your own order on the planning page):

| Component | ~ time |
|-----------|--------|
| The aim of the flight | 1–2 min |
| Today's flight — whiteboard walk-through | 4–5 min |
| The safety brief and the actions — rehearse them now | 4–5 min |
| Threats and how we'll manage them | 2–3 min |
| Questions, then fly | 1–2 min |

The order above puts the rehearsal right after the walk-through, on purpose — for this lesson it **is** the main threat mitigation.

**Administration** — the syllabus also asks you to schedule the **pre-solo examination** in this lesson, and the general English language proficiency assessment if it applies (CASR 141.306). Lesson 10 is the pre-solo circuit check, so this is the time to book both.

# The aim of the flight

**Ask** — "From the theory: what are we setting out to do today?"

**Expect** — in the student's own words, the purpose first:

> If the engine fails close to the ground, **keep flying the aeroplane** and land it under control — on the runway if we can reach it, ahead of us if we can't.

Then how today's flight builds toward it:

- recognise an abnormal take-off and **reject** it
- give a **pre-take-off safety brief** before every take-off
- fly the **engine failure after take-off** actions from memory, landing ahead
- handle an **engine failure in the circuit**, and fly a **glide approach** to the runway, correcting for high and low

Write the aim on the board as that one line — the four points are the *how*. Say it plainly rather than dramatically: the student has already flown every piece of this (climbs, glides, approaches, go-arounds); today joins the pieces together without power.

# Today's flight — whiteboard walk-through

**Draw** — the circuit, with the runway, the circuit direction and the ground beyond each runway end. Fill in your local details:

- Runway: ______ &nbsp;&nbsp; Circuit direction: ______ &nbsp;&nbsp; Circuit height: ______ ft
- Beyond the upwind end — best areas to land ahead: ______
- Best glide speed: ______ kt &nbsp;&nbsp; Turn-back height (if your operator has one): ______ ft
- Minimum height for a simulated engine failure after take-off: ______ ft AGL, and for the go-around from it: ______ ft AGL (per your operator)

**Draw** — the exercises as a numbered list, top to bottom:

1. **Engine start malfunctions** — talk through a flooded start and a fire on start-up during the start (introduced, not assessed)
2. **Rejected take-off** — simulated early in the roll, with plenty of runway, as your operator allows
3. **Engine failure after take-off** — simulated at a safe height on climb-out: nose down, area ahead, actions, then go around
4. **Engine failure in the circuit** — simulated on downwind or base, glide approach to the runway and land
5. **Glide approaches** — repeat from the downwind, correcting for high and low
6. A normal circuit and landing to finish

**Ask** — "Who's flying each part?"

**Expect** — you demonstrate the first of each, then hand over. **Say it before the flight, and again in the air: "Simulated" means the engine is fine.** Every simulated failure starts with the word "simulated", and you take control and go around at the briefed height if anything looks wrong.

# The safety brief and the actions — rehearse them now

The actions have to be **automatic** in the air, so build them on the ground first. Walk them through and have the student say them back before you leave the room.

**Ask** — "Give me your safety brief for today's runway."

**Expect** — the take-off roll (close the throttle and stop); runway remaining (land on it); no runway remaining (nose down, glide at ______ kt, land within about 30° of the nose); the turn-back height. Specific to today's runway and wind — not a recital.

**Draw** — the engine failure after take-off actions as steps on the board:

1. **Aviate** — **lower the nose** to the glide attitude, best glide speed
2. **Navigate** — land **ahead**, within about 30° either side; wings level by about 200 ft
3. **Communicate** — MAYDAY, only if there's time

Then, as time permits: change tanks or the quick checks, then fuel, ignition and master off, doors unlatched.

**Ask** — "Engine stops at 400 ft on climb-out. What's the first thing your hands do?"

**Expect** — **the nose comes down.** Not the radio, not the fuel selector, not looking for the runway behind. The airspeed starts dropping the moment the thrust stops.

**Normalise the surprise** — tell them plainly what a simulated failure feels like: the throttle comes back without warning, the noise drops, and the nose-high climb attitude suddenly looks very wrong. **That's normal**, and a moment of freeze is normal too — which is why we rehearse the first action until it's a reflex. Add: *if at any point you're uncomfortable, say so — we'll go around, talk it through, and try again.*

# Threats and how we'll manage them

I'M SAFE and PAVE are standing checks before **every** flight — by now the student runs them, rather than being taught them.

**Ask** — "Run me through your I'M SAFE — any flags today?"

**Expect** — honest self-assessment. Nerves before an engine-failure lesson are normal.

**Ask** — "Apply PAVE to today's flight — what stands out?"

**Expect** — Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, External. Listen for the **enVironment** angle — the wind (it changes every glide), the ground beyond the runway ends, and other circuit traffic, which a glide approach gives no right of way over.

**Ask** — apply **TEAM** to each: "What are our options?"

- **Transfer** · **Eliminate** · **Accept** · **Mitigate**

Likely ones to surface: **low-level simulated failures** (mitigate — briefed minimum heights, instructor go-around call, "simulated" every time); **startle** on the first one (mitigate — rehearsed, and you know it's coming); **other traffic** during a glide approach (mitigate — radio calls, lookout, and a go-around if the spacing goes); **an engine that's slow to respond** after a long glide at idle (mitigate — carburettor heat, and clearing the engine during the descent per your operator).

# Questions, then fly

**Ask** — "Any questions before we head out?"

Quick recap as you pack up the whiteboard:

- **Safety brief** before every take-off — out loud, for today's runway
- After take-off: **nose down first**, then land **ahead**
- In the circuit: the runway only if you can **reach it with margin**
- Glide approach: aim **a third of the way in**, flap only when it's **assured**

Confirm who handles the taxi, and go flying.
