# Language choices

> **"decide now"** — reinforces acting on the judgement, not just recognising it.

> **"keep the field in sight"** — repeated throughout the search and inspection.

> **"PAN early"** — say it as soon as the decision is made, before the first pass — not once you're low and busy, or once things have already got worse.

# Before starting the plane

- Confirm the student's **fuel log**, **best-endurance calculations**, and the **pre-flight documents** (MEL, NOTAMs, GNSS RAIM, ERSA, AIP) for today's flight are complete and correct — assessed this lesson (C2.1, C4.2)
- Confirm **approved practice areas/fields** for today, and the operator's **minimum height for a go-around** from a simulated precautionary landing — brief this explicitly, it is never flown to an actual touchdown outside an approved area or aerodrome unless a genuine short-field landing there is part of your operator's procedures
- **Cargo task** — hand the student a couple of small boxes and ask them to load them: "We're dropping some batteries off at another aerodrome while we're out." Let them work out where they go, how they're secured, and whether they're dangerous goods — assessed this lesson (C5.3)

# Simulated precautionary landing — instructor demonstration

## Sequence
- In the training area, declare the scenario without warning: "Simulated deteriorating weather" (or "Simulated low fuel") — "Simulated precautionary landing"
- **Decide** — narrate the decision out loud, tied to the simulated trigger
- **Configure** — slow, safe cruise, some flap, appropriate height
- **Select** — search the area and choose the field, narrating the WOSSSSET criteria out loud — approach/overshoot paths, length, surface
- **Communicate** — simulate the PAN call to ATC (do not transmit unless genuinely required)
- **Prepare** — narrate the passenger brief — both done before the first pass, not squeezed in during it
- **Inspect** — join on the normal crosswind at 1000 ft trimmed for 80 kt, and time the field on downwind (10 s ≈ 400 m, 20 s ≈ 800 m). Then a wider 500 ft pass: time again and average the two timings (or use the GNSS groundspeed instead), and assess WOSSSSET again — aim for at least 400 m, and check the aircraft manual's short-field landing distance. Climb back to the 1000 ft circuit height after each pass. Then the first low pass at ~200 ft to the right of the landing path, with full power applied two-thirds of the way along the runway, circuit, second (dummy) pass at ~50–100 ft to the right (full power two-thirds along again), re-check
- Fly the short-field landing, or go around no lower than the briefed minimum height

# Simulated precautionary landing — student flown

## Sequence
- Repeat from varying positions relative to the training area/approved fields, and with both simulated triggers (weather, low fuel)
- Student flies the full vital-actions sequence from memory: decide, configure, select, communicate, prepare, inspect, land
- Prompt only if a step is missed or out of order — in particular the PAN call and passenger brief must be done before the first pass — let the sequence run uninterrupted otherwise
- Debrief briefly after each repetition: what worked, what would you do differently?

# Short-field landing — revision

## Sequence
- At the chosen/approved field: full flap, stable approach, aim point chosen short of obstacles
- Airspeed with elevator, descent rate with throttle — the standard approach split
- Aim to reach the minimum recommended speed crossing the boundary
- Minimal float, touch down precisely, then maximum braking without locking the wheels
- Go around without hesitation if the approach is unstable, too high, or too fast

# Other abnormal situations (simulated)

## Sequence
- Simulate a low-fuel situation or deteriorating weather distinct from the main exercise — e.g. a fuel-quantity discrepancy noticed in cruise, or a sudden reduction in visibility
- Student correctly identifies the situation and maintains safe control of the aeroplane throughout
- Student manages the situation in accordance with relevant procedures — which may mean triggering the full precautionary-landing sequence above, or a lesser response (diversion, altitude change) if appropriate

# Return to aerodrome

- Establish cruise on descent back toward the aerodrome
- **Listen to AWIS/ATIS** — runway in use, circuit direction, QNH
- Fly a normal circuit and landing
- Complete the post-flight actions and administration (C2.3) at shutdown
