# Using these notes

The long brief is done; this briefing reviews it and turns it into today's flight. CASA allows about **30 minutes**: **ask, draw, and listen** rather than present, with the student's chart and log on the table.

**The components** (choose your own order on the planning page):

| Component | ~ time |
|-----------|--------|
| The aim of the flight | 2 min |
| Checking the plan | 5 min |
| Today's flight — whiteboard walk-through | 8–10 min |
| Controlled airspace and aerodrome — *controlled airspace module* | 5 min |
| Threats and how we'll manage them | 4–5 min |
| Airmanship emphasis — decide early | 3 min |
| Questions, then fly | 2 min |

Pages marked **— controlled airspace module** are for a PPL student, or an RPL holder adding the controlled aerodrome and controlled airspace endorsements. **Skip them** for a student training for the RPL navigation endorsement alone, or when today's route stays in Class G.

## The aim of the flight

**Ask** — "From the long brief: what's different about today?"

**Expect** — in the student's own words:

- today the plan **changes on purpose**: a diversion, a lost procedure, low level and weather, and flying for range and endurance
- the student **flies and navigates**, and makes the decisions
- *controlled airspace module:* a first experience of **controlled airspace** and a **controlled aerodrome**

Write the aim on the board as one line: *when the plan changes, decide early and fly the new one*.

# Checking the plan

The student planned today's route; check it together, don't redo it.

**Ask** — "Where are your options on this route?"

**Expect** — the planned alternates, en-route alternates and decision points marked on the chart: turn-back point, fuel check points, last-light check.

**Check together:**

- Latest forecasts and NOTAMs — **go / no-go:** ______ &nbsp; Alternate or holding fuel needed? ______
- Last light: ______ &nbsp;&nbsp; Planned landing back: ______
- Fuel required: ______ (including ______ holding / extra) &nbsp;&nbsp; On board: ______
- Range power setting and speed: ______ &nbsp;&nbsp; Endurance: ______
- Flight notification lodged / SARTIME: ______
- GNSS: database current, RAIM available, flight plan loaded and checked

# Today's flight — whiteboard walk-through

**Draw** — the route as a strip map down the board: departure → each leg → away aerodrome → return. Then mark where each scenario will happen:

| Scenario | Where on the route |
|----------|--------------------|
| Range, then endurance (holding) | ______ |
| Low-level navigation (≥ 500 ft AGL) | ______ |
| Lost procedure | ______ |
| Diversion | ______ → ______ |
| Basic instrument flight revision | ______ |
| Engine failure en route / turbulence / radio failure (talk-through) | ______ |

**Ask** — "When I say 'divert to ______', what do you do first?"

**Expect** — *decide, estimate, refine, tell*: say it, turn onto a rough heading now, then measure it, then tell ATS.

**Ask** — "And if nothing on the ground matches the map?"

**Expect** — *admit, check, search, act*: say it, check the last fix and the DI, draw the circle of uncertainty and read ground to map, then ask for help or head for a line feature.

**Agree** — the GNSS rule for today: ______

# Controlled airspace and aerodrome — controlled airspace module

Skip this page if the student isn't doing the controlled airspace module, or the route stays in Class G.

**Draw** — the controlled airspace on the route: the class, the steps' lower limits, the entry and exit points, and the frequencies.

- Controlled aerodrome: ______ &nbsp; Runway: ______ &nbsp; ATIS frequency: ______
- Inbound VFR approach point: ______ &nbsp; Call before: ______
- Taxi route and **hotspots**: ______

**Ask** — "Talk me through your calls, from the ATIS to the landing clearance."

**Expect** — ATIS first, then the inbound call well before the boundary (with the ATIS letter); remain outside until cleared; read back the clearance; join as instructed; confirm the landing clearance; vacate and get a taxi clearance.

**Ask** — "What if Tower says 'remain outside controlled airspace'?"

**Expect** — remain outside: hold over a known feature (endurance), or follow a plan B the student already has. And "unable" if anything can't be complied with.

# Threats and how we'll manage them

I'M SAFE and PAVE are standing checks before **every** flight — by now the student runs them, rather than being taught them.

**Ask** — "Run me through your I'M SAFE — any flags today?"

**Expect** — honest self-assessment. A long lesson with simulated problems is tiring; fatigue is a fair answer.

**Ask** — "Apply PAVE to today's flight — what stands out?"

**Expect** — Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, External. Listen for the **enVironment** angle (weather on the route, terrain and obstacles for the low-level leg) and the **External** one (the urge to complete the lesson as planned).

**Ask** — apply **TEAM** to each: "What are our options?"

- **Transfer** · **Eliminate** · **Accept** · **Mitigate**

Likely ones to surface: **obstacles and wires on the low-level leg** (mitigate — the leg chosen over known terrain, the chart studied for obstructions, 500 ft AGL as a hard floor); **real weather mixed with simulated weather** (mitigate — "simulated" said every time, and real weather always wins); **head-down time during the lost procedure and diversion** (mitigate — the instructor keeps the lookout, and the student holds the heading first); **controlled airspace infringement** (mitigate — boundaries on the chart and the strip map; no clearance, no entry).

# Airmanship emphasis — decide early

Today's real lesson is **judgement**: every scenario is a decision that gets harder the longer it's put off.

**Ask** — "What's the first step of both the diversion and the lost procedure?"

**Expect** — *say it out loud*. "I'm diverting." "I'm unsure of my position." Deciding is the hard part; the procedure follows.

**Ask** — "Who are you allowed to ask for help, and when?"

**Expect** — ATS, early; not as a last resort. And the instructor, in the aeroplane today.

**Normalise the surprise** — tell the student plainly: at some point today you'll be **unsure of your position**, either because I've made you so or because it happens. **That's normal, and exactly what the procedure is for.** Say it out loud, hold your heading, and work the steps. Nobody is judged for being unsure; only for not saying so.

# Questions, then fly

**Ask** — "Any questions before we head out?"

Quick recap as you pack up the whiteboard:

- **Diversion** — decide, estimate, refine, tell
- **Lost** — admit, check, search, act — ask early
- **Weather** — turn back while it's still an option; never below 500 ft AGL
- **Range** for distance, **endurance** for time — power, attitude, trim, lean
- *Controlled airspace module:* **no clearance, no entry**; read back; "unable" is fine

Confirm walking out: chart, log, ERSA, computer, pencil, hood for the instrument flight; GNSS set up per today's rule; who flies the first leg: ______.

Go flying.
