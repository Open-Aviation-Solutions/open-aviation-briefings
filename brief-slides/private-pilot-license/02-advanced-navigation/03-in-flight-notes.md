# Language choices

> **"simulated"** — before every scenario: "simulated weather ahead", "simulated radio failure". Real weather and real problems always win.

> **"say it out loud"** — the first step of every scenario: "I'm diverting", "I'm unsure of my position".

> **"where should we be now?"** — rather than "where are we?", which invites hunting the ground.

> **"what are your options?"** — hand the decision back before offering one.

<!--

CASA HF/NTS: task management [NTS1 & NTS2 4(b)]; plans of action [NTS2 4(d)]. The student makes the decisions today. "What are your options?" keeps the judgement with them; save direct instructions for safety.

-->

# Before starting the plane

- The daily inspection is done before the student arrives, unhurried — or walked together as a visible block at the start of a first-slot lesson. Today's flight is long: the fuel check and the fuel on board matter more than usual
- Student's **chart, log, forecasts, NOTAMs and ERSA** checked; **alternate / holding / extra fuel** decided; last light noted
- **Flight notification / SARTIME** lodged (or held per your operator's procedure): ______
- **Range and endurance** power settings and speeds on the student's kneeboard
- **GNSS** — database, RAIM, flight plan loaded and checked; today's GNSS rule agreed (NAV.8)
- **Hood** (or screen) on board for the instrument flight revision
- Cockpit organised so chart, log, computer and ERSA are reachable from the seat

# Departure — non-towered aerodrome

## Sequence
- Taxi, line-up and departure calls as in Lesson 01 (ONTA.2, ONTA.3)
- Set heading overhead or at the departure point; **log the time**
- **Gross error check**; established on track within **5 NM**; ETA for the first checkpoint
- Student flies **and** navigates from the start today; step in only to help with workload

<!--

CASA ONTA.2, ONTA.3, NAV.3. FIM Ch 16: on the second and later navigation flights, the student does both the flying and the navigating.

If departing from a controlled aerodrome instead, use the next page.

-->

# Departure — controlled aerodrome — controlled airspace module

## Sequence
- **ATIS** before calling; taxi call with the ATIS letter; **read back** the taxi clearance, including any hold short
- Taxi with the aerodrome chart; stop at every hold-short line not cleared past; **hotspots** identified; strobes on to cross a runway
- At the holding point: checks, then **"ready"**; transponder ALT before entering the runway
- Take-off only when cleared; fly the **departure instructions** exactly; watch for wake turbulence
- Airborne report / frequency change as instructed; tolerances held until clear of the control zone

<!--

CASA CTR.1, CTR.2(a–h), CTR.3(a–i). Several are at standard 3: help with the radio if the workload spikes, and note it for the debrief rather than taking over.

Common student error: reading back only part of a taxi clearance, or crossing a hold-short line because the runway "looks clear". Stop the aeroplane if there's any doubt about where it's cleared to.

-->

# En route — revision and instrument flight

## Sequence
- First leg: work cycle (time, map, ground), log, revised ETAs (±2 min), FREDA — as in Lesson 01
- **Pre-descent and turning point checks** at each turning point
- At a suitable point, clear of terrain and traffic: **basic instrument flight revision** under the hood — straight and level, a climb, a descent (about **0.3 hr**)
- I keep the lookout throughout the instrument flight; hood off before any scenario that needs map reading

<!--

CASA NAV.4, IFF.2 (straight and level, climbing and descending on the full instrument panel — revision of RPL Lesson 26).

The instrument flight is also a natural way into the lost procedure: after a few minutes under the hood with turns, the student can come out of it unsure of position (FIM Ch 16 suggests simulated instrument flight as one way to create the uncertainty).

-->

# Range and endurance

## Sequence — range
- On a long leg: "Set up for maximum range"
- Student: range **power** → hold height as the speed settles → **trim** → **lean** per the flight manual
- Note the speed, RPM and fuel flow; compare the groundspeed with the planned cruise

## Sequence — endurance (holding)
- Over a known feature clear of traffic: "Simulated — we need to hold here for 20 minutes"
- Student: endurance power → hold height → trim → lean; set up an **orbit** over the feature
- Note the fuel flow against cruise; work out how long we *could* hold with the fuel on board
- Watch the speed — endurance is close to slow flight; stall warning awareness

<!--

CASA A3.2 (straight and level at maximum range and maximum endurance), NAV.4 (configure for holding and maximum range). FIM Ch 16: the diversion exercise is a good place to fly for range, and simulated holding near the alternate long enough to set up the endurance configuration.

It's efficient to combine these with the diversion: fly the diversion leg for range, then hold for endurance near the diversion aerodrome.

-->

---

# Lost procedure

## Sequence
- If the student becomes unsure of position naturally — use it. Otherwise, after the hood or with the map withheld for a while: "Simulated — you're unsure of your position"
- **Admit** — student says it out loud, **holds the heading**
- **Check** — last positive fix, headings and times since; DI vs compass; variation and drift; track on the map; anything near the compass
- **Search** — DR position; circle of uncertainty (±10% of distance since the fix); read **ground to map**, biggest features first; confirm with two or more
- **Act** if still unsure — line feature, climb, reverse heading, or a simulated call to ATS for help; set up for **range**
- Discuss: at what point would you start a **precautionary search and landing**?

<!--

CASA NAV.6(a–f). FIM Ch 16: early on, let the student keep the log of headings and times through the exercise; later, make them fix position without it. If still not fixed by ETA plus 10% of the time since the last fix, demonstrate turning towards a prominent line feature.

Tip: use the GNSS (if fitted) at the end to confirm the fix, and ask the student why the uncertainty arose. It is usually a gross error, not bad map reading.

-->

# Low-level navigation

## Sequence
- Over terrain you've chosen in advance, clear of built-up and noise-sensitive areas: "Simulated — cloud base lowering to ______"
- Student: **bad-weather configuration** (speed, flap as the flight manual recommends, lights on)
- Descend to no lower than **500 ft AGL** — your briefed floor: ______ ft AMSL
- Navigate by the **2-minute marks** and features in profile; few log entries; overdue feature → assume passed
- Lookout for **wires, towers** and other aircraft; I watch the height and terrain throughout
- Then: "Simulated — weather worse ahead" — student decides and flies a **turn back** (a rate-one 180°)

<!--

CASA NAV.5(a–g) — at standard 3 for the low-level items today: introduced, not yet consistent. FIM Ch 16: at least 500 ft terrain clearance; stress the limited field of view and the need for anticipation and quick recognition; an overdue feature by DR is assumed passed.

Choose this leg's terrain and height in advance, and brief it as a hard floor. A turn at low level in a strong wind is where the downwind-turn illusion appears: watch the balance ball and the airspeed, not the ground.

-->

---

# Diversion

## Sequence
- Mid-leg, at a point you've planned: "Simulated — weather has closed in ahead. Divert to ______"
- **Decide** — student says it out loud
- **Estimate** — turns onto a rough heading **now**; logs the time
- **Refine** — draws the track; measures track and distance; heading with drift; ETA; fuel and daylight check
- **Tell** — simulated call to ATS (or a real one, per your operator): the diversion, the new ETA, an amended SARTIME
- Flies it like any leg — gross error check, first fix, revised ETA; **range** configuration on the way
- Near the diversion aerodrome: simulated holding for **endurance**, then rejoin the planned route or land

<!--

CASA NAV.7(a–e). FIM Ch 16: simulate deteriorating weather forcing an unplanned diversion; have the student pass a new heading and ETA, and fly for range on the leg; then hold long enough to set up the endurance configuration.

Measure the student's estimate against the refined heading and ETA: the first estimate should be within about 10° and a few minutes.

-->

# En-route emergencies

## Sequence — engine failure en route
- Throttle closed at a safe height over suitable terrain: "Simulated engine failure"
- **Aviate** — best glide, trim; **navigate** — field selection, using the chart to know what's around; **communicate** — simulated MAYDAY, squawk 7700 (talked, not set)
- Go around at the briefed height: ______ ft AGL

## Sequence — turbulence (talk-through, or if met)
- Turbulence speed: ______ kt; hold the attitude, not the height; harnesses tight

## Sequence — radio failure (talk-through)
- Check the obvious; **7600** (talked, not set); transmit blind; the ERSA procedure for where we are

<!--

CASA OGA (abnormal operations and emergencies; transponder emergency and communication failure codes; loss of radio communication), NAV.4 (configure for turbulence). The engine failure en route revises RPL Lesson 17; this time, the field selection should use the chart as well as what's visible.

Don't set 7700 or 7600 on the transponder for a simulation: say it and point to it.

-->

# In controlled airspace — controlled airspace module

## Sequence
- Before the boundary: **ATIS** if relevant; call with position, level and request; **remain outside** until cleared
- **Read back** the clearance; set the squawk; fly it within the **tracking and altitude tolerances**
- Practise a **"say again"** or **"confirm"** on purpose if the chance arises
- Simulated weather on the cleared route: student calls **"unable"** with a request for what they want instead
- Leaving: frequency change and squawk as instructed

<!--

CASA CTA.1(a–k), NAV.2(a–c) — CTA.1 is at standard 3 this lesson. Transiting a Class C or D zone, or a Class D aerodrome's control zone, is enough to cover most of it.

-->

---

# Arrival — controlled aerodrome — controlled airspace module

## Sequence
- ERSA and NOTAMs re-checked; **ATIS** before the inbound call; QNH set
- Inbound call before the VFR approach point — with the ATIS letter; **remain outside** until cleared
- Read back the clearance; join as instructed; keep looking out; update QNH
- **Confirm the landing clearance** before touchdown — none by short final: go around
- Vacate as directed; stop past the holding point; **taxi clearance**; read back

<!--

CASA CTR.4(a–j), CTR.2. The landing clearance check is the critical one: build the habit now of hearing and reading back "cleared to land" every time.

-->

---

# Arrival — non-towered aerodrome

## Sequence
- **Pre-descent checks**; ERSA and NOTAM re-checked; QNH set; CTAF selected
- Inbound call in good time; overfly if the wind, runway or circuit direction is unclear
- Join, circuit calls, land, vacate (ONTA.4, NAV.9)
- **Cancel or amend the SARTIME** before anything else once shut down

<!--

CASA NAV.9, ONTA.4. CASA (and FIR TE3-67) require a landing at an away aerodrome that isn't a major airport; if the controlled aerodrome is the away field, use this page for the return home.

-->

# Tips and common student errors

- **Deciding late** — pressing on into weather, or "giving it another minute" before admitting uncertainty. Ask "what are your options?" early
- **Hunting the ground** — wandering towards look-alikes instead of holding the heading; ask "where should we be now?"
- **Over-refining the diversion** — measuring while still flying the old heading. Turn first, then refine
- **Forgetting to tell** — no call to ATS, no amended SARTIME after a diversion
- **Head down at low level** — the log can wait; terrain can't
- **Slow and unaware** — endurance speed drifting towards the stall while the student's busy
- *Controlled airspace module:* entering without a clearance because "I called them"; partial readbacks
- Plan the scenarios so each has the time and space it needs; don't stack them all at the end

<!--

FIR TE3-67 guidance: plan the lesson so each activity, particularly the lost procedure and low-level navigation, has adequate time and positioning. FIM Ch 16: the student must not neglect lookout and accurate flying while engrossed in navigation.

-->

# Before you part — introduce the next lesson

Two or three minutes at the end of the debrief, no more — the point is that the student leaves knowing what is coming, and what to do before then.

**Next: PPL Lesson 03 — Navigation exercise 3.**

- **What it is** — another dual navigation flight, with circuits at an away aerodrome. It checks the competencies from the RPL and the first two navigation flights, and decides whether you're ready for your **first solo navigation flight**
- **The link from today** — on a solo navigation flight, there's nobody else to decide. Everything you practised today (diverting, being unsure, turning back) is what you'd do alone
- **Before then** — [workbook / reading to send]; plan the next route yourself, including where you'd divert from each leg
