# Advanced Navigation
## Pre-lesson Workbook

_CASA Private Pilot License (Aeroplane) — CASA Sample Syllabus Lesson 02, Pre-lesson Workbook_

Complete this before your lesson — on your own, at your own pace. For each topic: watch a video or read the pages listed, then answer the questions in the space provided. Print this out, or write your answers in a notebook. Topics 1–5 are for everyone and take about 45 minutes. Topics 6–7 are the **controlled airspace module**: do them if your lesson includes controlled airspace (your instructor will tell you), and allow about 20 minutes more. We'll check your answers together at the start of the theory brief.

# 1. Alternates, holding and extra fuel

On your first navigation flight you planned enough fuel to get there and back with the final reserve. Sometimes the forecast says you need more: fuel to hold until the weather clears, or to reach somewhere else if you can't land where you planned.

**Watch (both — under four minutes together):**
- [Explaining the rules — VFR alternates](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k0YRU3v_gTY) — CASA, under a minute. When a VFR flight must nominate a destination alternate.
- [Explaining the rules — Fuel planning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aVVoCv-ywHc) — CASA, about 2 minutes. A refresher from Lesson 01, now with holding and alternate fuel in mind.

**Read:**
- CASA VFRG — [Alternate aerodrome due to weather, p. 79](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=91) and the [alternate minima, p. 82](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=94); then the [fuel components, p. 99](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=111).
- Optional: CASA's [sample fuel calculation](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-08/advisory-circular-91-15-annex-a-sample-fuel-calculations-single-engine-piston-aeroplane.PDF) — a worked example with trip, alternate, holding, final reserve and additional fuel.

**Answer:**

1. For a VFR flight, name two forecast conditions at your destination that mean you must plan an **alternate** aerodrome: ________________________________

2. The destination TAF has a **TEMPO** below the alternate minima. Instead of an alternate, what can you carry, and how much? What about an **INTER**? ________________________________

3. Using real figures for your training aircraft: how many litres is 30 minutes of holding fuel? ________________________________

4. Food for thought: list three reasons, not in the rules, why you might choose to carry **more** fuel than the minimum on a navigation flight: ________________________________

# 2. Range and endurance

Sometimes you need to go as **far** as possible on the fuel you have. Other times you need to stay up as **long** as possible. They're different, and they're flown at different speeds.

**Watch (both):**
- [Range and Endurance Speeds Explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_WAzslKkpGs) — UKGroundSchool, under 2 minutes. Where the two speeds sit on the power-required curve.
- [PPL Flight Training Exercise 10 — Range and Endurance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oXQ6_HvxbLA) — Flight Instruction 101, about 9 minutes. The air exercise you'll fly: setting up each configuration in the aeroplane. It's Canadian, but the aerodynamics and the technique are the same here.

**Read:**
- CASA VFRG — the [worked example, pp. 106–108](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=118), which reads a real cruise table for power, speed and fuel flow.

**Answer:**

1. In one sentence each, define **maximum range** and **maximum endurance**: ________________________________

2. Which is flown at the slower speed? Why? ________________________________

3. Why should you fly a little **faster** than the best range speed into a strong headwind? And why does the wind make **no difference** to endurance? ________________________________

4. Find your training aircraft's flight manual cruise or range chart. Write down the power setting and the speed it gives for the best range, and where in the manual you found it: ________________________________

5. Name one situation on a navigation flight where you'd fly for range, and one where you'd fly for endurance: ________________________________

# 3. Low level and the weather

When the cloud comes down, the pressure is to come down with it. Knowing the rules for how low you may fly, and how much you need to see, tells you when to stop and turn back.

**Watch (all three — about 5 minutes together):**
- [Explaining the rules — minimum heights](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rsz5oV9mT9A) — CASA, under a minute.
- [Explaining the rules — VMC criteria](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGuL5i2M_v8) — CASA, about a minute.
- ['178 seconds to live' — updated](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xTzVpRLrQB8) — CASA, about 3 minutes. A recreation of a VFR pilot flying into cloud. Watch it to the end.

**Read:**
- CASA VFRG — minimum heights over [populous areas, p. 44](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=56) and [other areas, p. 47](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=59); the [VMC criteria, pp. 204–206](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=216); and [VFR flight into IMC, p. 435](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=447).

**Answer:**

1. What's the minimum height you may fly over a **populous area**, and over other areas? ________________________________

2. In Class G below 3,000 ft AMSL (or 1,000 ft AGL), what visibility and distance from cloud do you need for VFR? ________________________________

3. Name three hazards of flying at low level that you don't meet at normal cruising height: ________________________________

4. Food for thought: why do so many VFR-into-cloud accidents happen to pilots who could see clearly a few minutes earlier? What will you do to stop it happening to you? ________________________________

# 4. GNSS — a supplement, not a substitute

Most aeroplanes — and every phone — have GNSS (GPS). It's a great navigation aid, but for a VFR pilot it supports map reading and dead reckoning. It doesn't replace them.

**Read:**
- CASA VFRG — [VFR navigation requirements and GNSS, pp. 202–203](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=214).

**Watch (pick one):**
- [RAIM Explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=To9aHYOeWDk) — ATPL Student, about 6 minutes. How GNSS fixes a position, and how RAIM checks it.
- [What is RAIM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ESZcUCKieA) — FlightInsight, about 4 minutes. Clear on the principles; it's American, so ignore the US rules it mentions.

**Optional, for more depth:**
- [Use of Navaids and GNSS in VFR Navigation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XgOh2Rsx2k4) — Aviation Theory, about 18 minutes.

**Answer:**

1. In a sentence: how does a GNSS receiver work out its position? ________________________________

2. What does **RAIM** check, and why does it matter? ________________________________

3. Name two ways a GNSS can lead you astray: ________________________________

4. Your GNSS says you're 3 NM left of track. The map, the clock and the ground say you're on it. What do you do? ________________________________

# 5. When the radio goes quiet

Radios fail — often just a volume knob, a loose headset plug or the wrong frequency. When it's a real failure, there's a set procedure, and a transponder code that tells ATC about it without a word.

**Watch (both):**
- [What to do when your radio fails — GoFly Fix](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lzxz8AzNO0U) — GoFly Online, about 3 minutes. An Australian instructor walks through the procedure.
- [How to operate the transponder — GoFly Fix](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aA-Oj9PEhtk) — GoFly Online, about 4 minutes. Including the emergency codes.

**Read:**
- CASA VFRG — [transponder emergency codes, p. 234](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=246) and [radio failure en route in Class G and E, p. 362](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=374).

**Answer:**

1. What are the first three things you'd check if the radio seems to have stopped working? ________________________________

2. What do you **squawk** for radio failure? For an emergency? ________________________________

3. Find the radio failure procedures in the **ERSA** (EMERG section). Summarise what a VFR pilot in **Class G** should do: ________________________________

4. What does "transmitting blind" mean, and why would you do it? ________________________________

# 6. Controlled airspace — controlled airspace module

*Skip this topic if your lesson doesn't include controlled airspace.*

So far you've flown only in Class G. Controlled airspace has its own rules for VFR flights: when you need a clearance, what ATC does for you, and what you must do for ATC.

**Watch (all three — about 8 minutes together):**
- [Explaining the rules — Classes of airspace](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tUyF7ioUs6Q) — CASA, under 3 minutes.
- [Explaining the rules — Controlled airspace communication](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PI1YOV0v9UQ) — CASA, about a minute. Listening watch and clearances.
- [Introduction to Controlled Airspace](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1QCgvEcnkbQ) — GoFly Online, about 4 minutes. Flying into Class D and C.

**Read:**
- CASA VFRG — [airspace classification, p. 220](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=232), the [VMC criteria, pp. 205–206](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=217) and the [read-back requirements, p. 378](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=390).
- Airservices — [Operating in Class D airspace](https://www.airservicesaustralia.com/wp-content/uploads/Safety-Net-Operating-in-Class-D-Airspace.pdf), a two-page fact sheet.

**Answer:**

1. Which classes of airspace are used in Australia? Which one is around most towered GA aerodromes? ________________________________

2. In which classes does a VFR flight need a **clearance**? ________________________________

3. What VMC (visibility and distance from cloud) apply in Class C and Class D? How do they differ from Class G? ________________________________

4. Name five items in a clearance or instruction that you must **read back**: ________________________________

# 7. Towered aerodromes — controlled airspace module

*Skip this topic if your lesson doesn't include a controlled aerodrome.*

At a towered aerodrome, you need a clearance to taxi, to enter or cross a runway, to take off and to land. Most mistakes there happen on the ground.

**Watch (all three — about 12 minutes together):**
- [Pilot Runway Safety](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ppZwmGIpbkE) — Airservices Australia, about 5½ minutes. How runway incursions happen, and how to avoid them.
- [Out-n-Back — Episode 19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9em0c2DM0wA) — CASA, about 5 minutes. Readbacks and radio procedures at a towered aerodrome.
- [Explaining the rules — Light signals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y_Fo-UjzQaE) — CASA, under 1½ minutes.

**Read:**
- CASA VFRG — [engine start, taxi and ATIS, p. 268](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=280) and [light and ground signals, p. 64](https://www.casa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-02/visual-flight-rules-guide.pdf#page=76).
- Airservices — [A pilot's guide to runway safety](https://airservicesaustralia.com/wp-content/uploads/A-PilotsGuide-RunwaySafety.pdf) — skim the taxi and hotspot sections (a large PDF).

**Answer:**

1. What is the **ATIS**, and how do you tell ATC you've received it? ________________________________

2. What's a **hotspot** on an aerodrome chart? Find one at the controlled aerodrome nearest you, and describe why it's there: ________________________________

3. You're taxiing and the radio fails. What do these light signals from the tower mean: steady green, flashing green, steady red, flashing red? ________________________________

4. On a sheet of paper, write out your radio calls for a departure from a controlled aerodrome near you: ATIS, taxi, ready, airborne. Bring it to the lesson.

# Bring this to your lesson

- Bring your completed answers (printed, or your notebook) to the theory brief
- We'll check through them together and answer anything that didn't make sense
- From there, the live theory brief builds on this: what to do when the plan changes — diverting, the lost procedure, low level, range and endurance — and, if your lesson includes it, flying in controlled airspace and at a controlled aerodrome

> The time to learn what to do when things go wrong is on the ground, with a cup of tea, not in the air with the cloud coming down.
