# A new kind of radio call

- In Class G, you **tell** people what you're doing. What changes in controlled airspace?
- You **ask** — and then do **exactly** what you're cleared to do, no more, no less

> A clearance is an agreement. ATC plans around you keeping it.

<!--

# Learning objectives for this part

By the end of this session, our aim is to be able to:

- **Identify** the classes of controlled airspace on the chart, and **state** what each requires of a VFR flight
- **Request**, **read back** and **comply with** a clearance — and **say** what to do if you can't
- **Describe** the departure from a controlled aerodrome: ATIS, taxi, holding point, take-off, departure
- **Describe** the arrival at a controlled aerodrome: ATIS, inbound call, clearance, joining, landing, taxi
- **State** the actions for radio failure in controlled airspace

And then later in our actual flight: we'll operate in controlled airspace and at a controlled aerodrome on today's route.

# The classes, for a VFR flight

| Class | Clearance for VFR? | Separation for VFR |
| ----- | ------------------ | ------------------ |
| **A** | VFR not permitted | — |
| **C** | **Yes** | Separated from IFR; traffic information on other VFR |
| **D** | **Yes** | Traffic information on all traffic |
| **E** | No — but transponder and radio listening watch | Traffic information as far as practicable |
| **G** | No | None — see and avoid, radio calls |

- On the chart: the **lower limits** of each step, and the **frequencies** for each sector
- Check the **VMC** for each class in the VFRG — they differ from Class G

<!--

CASA CTA.1(a, i): comply with airways clearance requirements for operating in all classes of airspace; apply separation standards between IFR flights, and IFR and VFR flights, in the various classes of CTA. NAV.2(a): identify airspace restrictions and dimensions applicable to the flight.

Australian airspace doesn't use Class B or F. Class D surrounds most tower-controlled GA aerodromes (e.g. Bankstown, Moorabbin, Jandakot, Archerfield, Parafield) and many regional ones; Class C surrounds the major airports and sits above much of the Class D. Check these against the current AIP and VFRG before teaching, especially the VMC criteria for each class.

PHAK Ch 15 (Airspace) explains the classes, but the US system differs (it uses Class B, and its VFR rules in Class D and E differ). Use the Australian sources for the rules.

Reader instruction: bring up the VTC and VNC for today's route and point out each class boundary the route crosses or goes near.

# Getting a clearance

- **Plan it** — a flight plan with the route, level and SARTIME, lodged in good time; know where you'll enter and leave
- **Ask before the boundary** — call well before you reach it: who you are, where you are, what you want
- **No clearance, no entry** — if you haven't been cleared, **remain outside**, even if you've called
- **Listen for the limit** — a clearance takes you to a point, a level, a route. Beyond that, you need another

> "Remain outside controlled airspace" is a normal answer. Have a plan for it.

<!--

Required at standard 3.

# Read back, and keep it

**Read back** every clearance or instruction about:

- **Route**, **level** and **heading** — and **clearance limits**
- The **runway** — enter, cross, take off, land, hold short, backtrack
- **Frequencies**, **squawk codes** and **QNH**

Then **keep it**:

- Hold the **tracking and altitude tolerances** — ATC is relying on them
- **Not sure? Ask** — "say again" or "confirm"
- **Can't comply?** Say "**unable**" as soon as you know — e.g. cloud ahead on the cleared route

<!--

CASA CTA.1(c–g): maintain control area protection tolerances; maintain tracking and altitude tolerances; reconfirm any clearance item when in doubt; advise ATC as soon as possible if unable to maintain a clearance because of weather; follow ATC requirements for a change of level, including in an emergency. CTR.3(c): receive and correctly read back an airways clearance. NAV.4: maintain track within published tolerances in controlled airspace.

Check the full readback list in the AIP against the bullets above before teaching; the bullets are a summary.

The weather case links Part 1's turn-back decision: in controlled airspace, a turn back or diversion needs a call first (unless it's an emergency). Say "unable" early, with a request for what you'd like instead.

-->

# Radio failure in controlled airspace

- **Check the obvious** — volume, frequency, headset, push-to-talk; try the other radio
- **Squawk 7600**
- **Transmit blind** — position and intentions, in case they can hear you
- Follow the **ERSA radio failure procedure** for controlled airspace — know it before you go
- Watch the tower for **light signals** if you're at a controlled aerodrome

# Before you taxi

- **ERSA and NOTAMs** — runways, taxiways, **hotspots**, frequencies, VFR departure procedures
- **ATIS** — the runway, wind, QNH, and the **letter**; tell Ground "**received** ______"
- **Taxi clearance** — where to, by which route, and any **hold short** instructions. Read it back
- **Aerodrome chart** on your lap, and your finger on where you are

> Most runway incursions start with a pilot who wasn't sure where they were — or where they were cleared to.

<!--

# Taxi, holding point, take-off

| Stage | What happens |
| ----- | ------------ |
| **Taxi** | Follow the cleared route; **stop** at every hold-short line you're not cleared past; strobes on to cross a runway |
| **Holding point** | Run-up and pre-take-off checks; tell Tower you're **ready** |
| **Line-up and take-off** | Only when cleared; check the approach is clear anyway; transponder **ALT** before entering the runway |
| **Departure** | Fly the **departure instructions** exactly — heading, altitude, departure point — and watch for **wake turbulence** |
| **Leaving** | Report as instructed; change frequency when told; stay within tolerances until clear of the control zone |

- Not sure where you are, or where you're cleared to? **Stop and ask** — "request progressive taxi"

<!--

# Arriving at a controlled aerodrome

1. **Before you go** — ERSA and NOTAMs: the inbound **VFR approach points**, frequencies, the arrival procedure
2. **Before the boundary** — **ATIS** first; set the **QNH**; then call: who, where, received ______, request
3. **Clearance** — read it back, set the squawk, and **remain outside** until you have it
4. **Joining** — fly the instructions: which leg, which runway, any orbits or holding; **keep looking out** and stay in tolerance
5. **Landing** — **confirm the clearance to land** before you touch down. No clearance by short final? **Go around**
6. **After landing** — vacate as directed, stop past the holding point, and get a **taxi clearance**

<!--

CASA CTR.4(a–j): check ERSA and NOTAM before entering the control area; receive ATIS and set QNH; request and receive a clearance and set the transponder code before entering; advise ATC as soon as possible if unable to comply; maintain lookout; update QNH; maintain tracking tolerances; establish on the correct leg of the circuit and maintain separation; confirm the clearance to land; vacate the runway and obtain a taxi clearance.

Reader instruction: replace step 1's generic wording with today's actual inbound route and VFR approach point, and draw it on the whiteboard in the pre-flight brief.

Common student error: asking for a clearance with the ATIS still unread, or with the aeroplane already at the boundary. Plan the calls back from the boundary so the ATIS is done and the call is made with time to spare.
