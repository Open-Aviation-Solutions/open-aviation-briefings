# Language choices

> **"time, map, ground"** — the order for every fix; say it until the student says it first.

> **"where should we be now?"** — rather than "where are we?", which invites hunting the ground.

> **"heads up"** — a cue to finish the step and look out, not a criticism.

# Taxi and departure

- Taxi with the aerodrome chart if available; **broadcast** taxi intentions, obtain traffic information, watch for ground markings (ONTA.2)
- **Strobes** on when crossing any runway; transponder set **before** entering the runway for take-off
- Check the runway approaches are clear in **all directions** before lining up; **line-up broadcast** (ONTA.3)
- After take-off: set heading overhead or at the departure point — **log the time**
- Within the first few minutes: **gross error check** — DI vs compass, heading vs log, the ground vs the drift lines
- **Established on track within 5 NM**; ETA for the first checkpoint written down (NAV.3)

# First leg — instructor-led work cycle

## Sequence
- I fly the aeroplane; the student navigates
- At each checkpoint time, prompt the cycle: **time** (where should we be?) → **map** (what should we see, and which side?) → **ground** (find it, confirm the detail around it)
- Student logs the fix and time, and **revises the ETA** (±2 min)
- **FREDA** check; then **lookout** until the next cycle
- As the student copes, hand over the flying — first straight-and-level between checkpoints, then the whole leg

<!--

# Track error and correction

## Sequence
- At a suitable point on a leg, have the student **estimate the track error** from the drift lines, then check it with the **1-in-60** rule
- Apply **double the error for the same time**, then the original heading ± the track error
- Confirm regaining track at the next checkpoint; **revise the ETA**
- If the actual wind is well off the forecast: re-estimate drift with the **clock code**, and revise the heading and groundspeed on the log

scenario) to create a measurable track error.

-->

# Turning points and airspace

## Sequence
- **Before** the turning point: confirm the new heading, next leg time, and frequency
- **At** the turning point: turn onto the new heading, **log the time**, FREDA
- **After**: check the new track against the drift lines within the first few minutes
- Throughout: keep outside controlled airspace and active restricted areas — tracking and altitude tolerances; listening watch; calls as required (NAV.2, OGA)
- Use the **GNSS to confirm** a fix, not to find it (NAV.8)

# Arrival at the away aerodrome

## Sequence
- **Pre-descent checks**; ERSA and NOTAM re-checked; QNH set; CTAF selected
- **Inbound call** in good time — position, level, intentions, estimate
- **Overfly** at least 500 ft above circuit height if the wind, runway or circuit direction can't be determined — windsock, runway, traffic
- **Descend on the non-active side**; join and fly the circuit; all circuit calls
- Verify the runway is clear; land; vacate
- **Cancel the SARTIME** (or amend it for the return) — before anything else once shut down

<!--

Common student error: descending into the active side of the circuit from the overfly,

# Return leg and arrival home

## Sequence
- Departure from the away aerodrome as before — ONTA.3, NAV.3
- Student flies **and** navigates the return leg as the workload allows; instructor monitors
- Arrival home: pre-descent checks, AWIS/ATIS if available, inbound call, join, land
- **Cancel the SARTIME**; complete the post-flight actions and paperwork

<!--

Tip: on the return leg, step back as far as the student's workload allows — the
debrief is richer if the student has made (and caught) their own errors.

-->

# Tips and common student errors

- **Hunting the ground** — scanning for a match instead of reasoning from time and heading; ask "where should we be now?"
- **Turning towards look-alikes** — heading for anything that looks like the checkpoint; hold the heading, check the time, look for the next one
- **Head down too long** — log entries and calculations crowding out lookout and aircraft control; take the flying when it spikes
- **Heading wandering** while writing — the track error is often self-inflicted
- **DI not re-aligned** — a slowly precessing DI produces a steady, puzzling track error
- **Forgetting the SARTIME** at the away aerodrome
- Note debrief points in flight — tell the student beforehand that you'll be writing

<!--

# Before you part — introduce the next lesson

Two or three minutes at the end of the debrief, no more — the point is that the student leaves knowing what is coming, and what to do before then.

**Next: PPL Lesson 02 — Navigation exercise 2.**

- **What it is** — a second navigation flight that adds **controlled aerodromes and controlled airspace**, flying for **range and endurance**, **low-level navigation**, the **lost procedure** and **diversions**, plus turbulence, radio failure and a practice forced landing en route
- **The link from today** — today the clock, the log and the 1-in-60 rule kept you on track; next lesson asks what you do when they *don't* — a checkpoint that never appears, weather across the route, or needing to go somewhere you didn't plan
- **For RPL holders** — the low-level, lost-procedure and diversion items are the rest of the recreational navigation endorsement; the controlled-airspace items belong to the separate controlled aerodrome and controlled airspace endorsements
