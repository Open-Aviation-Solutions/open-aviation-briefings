# Learning objectives for this part

By the end of this session, our aim is to be able to:

- **Describe** how to depart on track and confirm there's no gross error
- **Explain** the navigation work cycle — time, map, ground — and the en-route checks
- **Calculate** a track error with the 1-in-60 rule, and the heading change to regain track
- **Outline** the procedures for operating in Class G and staying clear of controlled and restricted airspace
- **Describe** the arrival and join at a non-towered aerodrome

And then later in our actual flight: we'll fly today's route, with you navigating.

Revision bridge from Lesson 19: the clock code estimates the crosswind component on a

Demo script: leave the runway at 36 (think of it as a 360° track). Set the wind to 20 kt

# Departing on track

- **Set heading** — overhead the aerodrome or from a nominated departure point, and **log the time**
- **Early check** — within the first few minutes, confirm there's no gross error: DI aligned with the compass, heading matches the log, the ground matches the drift lines
- **Establish track** within 5 NM of the aerodrome
- **Calculate the ETA** for the first checkpoint — and write it down

> A gross error found in the first five minutes costs you one small correction. Found after thirty, it can cost you the flight.

# The navigation work cycle

Once established, navigation is a loop you repeat — not something you do continuously:

1. **Time** — how long since the last fix? Where *should* we be?
2. **Map** — what should we see there, and on which side?
3. **Ground** — look for it; confirm it with detail around it
4. **Log** — record the fix and time; revise the ETA
5. **Check** — the en-route checks, then **lookout** until the next cycle

> Map to ground, not ground to map. Know what you're looking for *before* you look.

<!--

CASA NAV.4(b): maintain a navigation cycle that ensures accurate tracking. NAV.4(e):

time — read from map to ground; having identified the feature, check the detail around
it. PHAK Ch 16 — Pilotage.

# En-route checks

At regular intervals — and at every turning point — run a **FREDA** check:

| | | Check |
| - | - | ----- |
| **F** | Fuel | Fuel remaining vs the log; tank selection; fuel pressure |
| **R** | Radio | Right frequency, volume, transponder code |
| **E** | Engine | Temperatures and pressures, mixture, carburettor heat |
| **D** | Directional indicator | Aligned with the compass |
| **A** | Altitude | Right level, altimeter setting |

**Turning point:** before the turn, confirm the new heading and time; after it, log the time and check the new track.

<!--

CASA NAV.4(h): perform pre-descent and turning point checks; NAV.4(j): monitor aircraft
systems, manage fuel and engine to achieve flight-plan objectives. FIM Ch 16: DI
alignment with the compass at frequent intervals. HF/NTS: use of checklists and
standard operating procedures to prevent errors [NTS2 4(h)].

FREDA is a widely used Australian en-route check, not a CASA-mandated one. Reader
instruction: use your operator's en-route, turning-point and pre-descent check format if
it differs.

-->

# Engine and fuel management

- **Mixture** — lean for the cruise as the flight manual recommends, and enrich it before descent
- **Carburettor heat** — check for icing regularly, especially in humid air and at reduced power
- **Fuel** — check the gauges and remaining time against the log at each FREDA; change tanks on schedule
- **Endurance vs the plan** — if the fuel check and the log disagree, find out why *now*

**From Lesson 24:** fuel starvation vs fuel exhaustion — both are prevented here.

# Off track — the 1-in-60 rule

**From your workbook:** 1 NM off track after 60 NM flown ≈ 1° of track error.

**Track error (°)** = $\dfrac{\text{distance off track} \times 60}{\text{distance flown}}$

- 3 NM off after 30 NM flown → **6°**
- 2 NM off after 20 NM flown → **6°**

> The drift lines on your chart do this for you — each one is 10° of track error.

<!--

The 10° drift lines drawn in Part 1 let the student read the track error straight off
the chart: halfway to a drift line is about 5°.

-->

---

# Getting back on track

Two ways to fix it — both start with the track error:

| Method | How |
| ------ | --- |
| **Double the error** | Alter heading by **twice** the track error for the **same time** it took to drift off; then fly the original heading ± the track error |
| **Track error + closing angle** | Closing angle = 1-in-60 on the **distance to go**; alter heading by track error + closing angle to reach the next checkpoint on track |

Then **revise the ETA** — within ±2 minutes.

<!--

FIM Ch 16: in the initial stages, brief the student to regain track by doubling the
track error and flying the new heading for the same time it took to build the error,
then flying the original heading plus or minus the track error. The track error +
closing angle method (1-in-60 applied to the distance remaining) is the more efficient
method once the student is comfortable — introduce both, expect the first today. CASA
NAV.4(d): maintain and revise ETAs (±2 minutes) for waypoint or destination.

# Using GNSS — as an aid

- A GPS or EFB moving map is a great **cross-check** — confirming a fix, a groundspeed, an ETA
- Today, the chart, log and clock are the **primary** method — the GNSS confirms, it doesn't lead
- Before relying on it at all: check the database is current and the receiver has integrity (**RAIM**, from Lesson 24)

> If the screen goes blank, you should still know exactly where you are.

<!--

# Operating in Class G

- **Stay clear** of controlled airspace and active restricted areas — keep a margin from each boundary, both laterally and vertically
- **Know the steps** — the base of controlled airspace often lowers as you approach a city
- **Listen out** on the area frequency; broadcast when it helps others know where you are
- **Transponder** on, squawking **1200**
- **Look out** — there's no one separating you from other VFR traffic

# Arriving at a non-towered aerodrome

1. **Before top of descent** — pre-descent checks; ERSA and NOTAMs re-checked; local or area QNH set
2. **Inbound call** — on the CTAF, in good time
3. **Overfly** — if the wind, runway or circuit direction can't be determined, overfly at least **500 ft above circuit height** and look at the windsock
4. **Descend on the non-active side**, and join as you learnt in the circuit lessons
5. **Look out, and give way** — to aircraft already in the circuit
6. **After landing** — vacate, then **cancel the SARTIME**

<!--

# Landing somewhere new

- **The picture is different** — runway width, slope, surroundings and circuit landmarks are all new
- **Surface and length** — confirmed from the ERSA, and again from the air
- **Go around** if anything doesn't look right — the same as at home
- **On the ground** — follow the ground markings; ask local traffic or the operator if unsure where to park

> An unfamiliar aerodrome is where good circuit habits pay off. Fly the same circuit you fly at home.
