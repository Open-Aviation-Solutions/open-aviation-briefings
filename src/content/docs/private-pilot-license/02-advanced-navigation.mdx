CASA Private Pilot License (Aeroplane) — CASA Sample Syllabus Lesson 02 (Navigation exercise 2). Also covers recreational navigation endorsement training for RPL holders, and the controlled aerodrome and controlled airspace endorsements (see below).

This lesson is about what to do when the plan changes. On the ground: planning alternates, holding and extra fuel, and the decision points on a route. In the air: flying for range and endurance, diverting, navigating at low level and in reduced visibility, the lost procedure, and the en-route emergencies (radio failure, turbulence, an engine failure en route), with a revision of basic instrument flight. For students who need it, it also introduces controlled airspace and controlled aerodromes. CASA suggests about 3 hours dual, with a landing at an away aerodrome.

## Which parts do you need?

The lesson is built in two halves, so it works for more than one kind of student. Everything about the **controlled airspace module** is clearly marked in each file, so you can skip it without losing anything else.

| Student | Core: when the plan changes | Controlled airspace module |
| ------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| PPL | ✓ | ✓ |
| RPL holder: recreational navigation endorsement | ✓ | — |
| RPL holder: navigation plus controlled aerodrome and controlled airspace endorsements | ✓ | ✓ |

- **Core** — workbook topics 1–5, Theory Part 1, and every page of the pre-flight and in-flight notes without the module label. This is the rest of the RPL recreational navigation endorsement after PPL Lesson 01: low-level and reduced-visibility navigation, the lost procedure and diversions (MOS NAV.5–7).
- **Controlled airspace module** — workbook topics 6–7, Theory Part 2, and the pages marked **— controlled airspace module** in the pre-flight and in-flight notes. It covers the controlled airspace (CTA.1) and controlled aerodrome (CTR.1–4) elements of the PPL, which are also the RPL controlled aerodrome and controlled airspace endorsements. Use it when the route includes controlled airspace.

This lesson pairs its theory brief with a self-paced **pre-lesson workbook**. Complete it before the lesson. The core topics cover alternates, holding and extra fuel; range and endurance; low level and the weather; GNSS as an aid; and radio failure. The controlled airspace module adds airspace classes and clearances, and towered aerodromes. The core takes about 45 minutes, plus about 20 for the module, and your answers get checked together at the start of the theory brief.

## Theory Brief — Part 1: When the Plan Changes

The live session builds on the workbook: planning options on the ground and choosing decision points; flying for range and endurance; diverting (decide, estimate, refine, tell); deteriorating weather and low-level navigation; the lost procedure (admit, check, search, act) and GNSS as a supplement; and the en-route emergencies. The pre-flight brief (~30 min, immediately before the flight) works best as an
interactive **whiteboard session**, not slides — checking the student's plan and its
options, drawing the route as a strip map with each scenario marked where it will
happen, and agreeing how the scenarios will run. These printable notes give you the
briefing components (aim, checking the plan, flight walk-through, the controlled
airspace module, threats, airmanship emphasis on deciding early) with prompts and
fill-in blanks, plus a front page to sketch your own whiteboard plan and running order.
Although it will be helpful to plan and step through it yourself before the lesson,
remember: **modelling the process** of the pre-flight brief is more important than
presenting a polished pre-flight brief. This is why we do it interactively rather than a
presentation.

## Useful resources for students

- The NZ CAA Flight Instructor Guide whiteboards each lay out one technique on a single page:
  [Weather avoidance and diversion](https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/licensing-and-certification/flight-instructor-guide/whiteboards/3.-Weather-avoidance-and-diversion.pdf),
  [Inadvertent IMC and lost procedures](https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/licensing-and-certification/flight-instructor-guide/whiteboards/4.-Inadvertent-IMC-and-lost-procedures.pdf),
  [Low flying](https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/licensing-and-certification/flight-instructor-guide/whiteboards/fig-whiteboard-am-lfi.pdf) and
  [Terrain and weather awareness](https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/licensing-and-certification/flight-instructor-guide/whiteboards/fig-whiteboard-am-twa.pdf). For the controlled
  airspace module, see [Landing away](https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/licensing-and-certification/flight-instructor-guide/whiteboards/2.-Landing-away.pdf), which covers arriving at
  a controlled aerodrome. These follow New Zealand procedures, so check the details
  against the Australian VFRG and ERSA.
- The ATSB's report [AB-2018-067](https://www.atsb.gov.au/occurrence-briefs/ab-2018-067)
  follows a student on a solo navigation flight who became unsure of their position,
  and shows why it pays to ask ATC for help early.
- The CASA [Visual Flight Rules Guide](https://www.casa.gov.au/resources-and-education/publications/industry-guides/pilot-guides-and-maps/visual-flight-rules-guide)
  is the Australian reference for alternates and fuel, minimum heights, radio failure,
  and controlled airspace and aerodromes.
- The FAA [Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/phak),
  Chapter 11 (Aircraft Performance) covers range and endurance, and Chapter 16
  (Navigation) covers diversions, lost procedures and GNSS. Its airspace chapter
  describes the US system, which differs from Australia's.

days before a lesson is a great way to engage people in the learning before they even arrive
(if they have time and capacity). Pick the version that matches the student.
