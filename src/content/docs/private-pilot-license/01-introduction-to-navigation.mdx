CASA Private Pilot License (Aeroplane) — CASA Sample Syllabus Lesson 01. Also covers
recreational navigation endorsement training for RPL holders (see below).

This lesson introduces pilot navigation and operations in Class G airspace: planning a
cross-country flight — weather and NOTAMs, route and chart preparation, cruising level,
last light, fuel, and the flight plan and SARTIME — and then flying it by dead reckoning
and map reading, correcting track errors with the 1-in-60 rule, and departing from and
arriving at non-towered aerodromes. The flight itself is about 2.5 hours dual, including
a landing at an away aerodrome.

**For RPL holders:** every competency assessed in this lesson (navigation, non-towered
aerodrome and Class G operations) is part of the RPL recreational navigation
endorsement, so the lesson serves endorsement training as it stands. The endorsement
also requires low-level and reduced-visibility navigation, the lost procedure and
diversions, which the CASA syllabus covers in the next PPL lesson.

This lesson pairs its theory brief with a self-paced **pre-lesson workbook** — complete
it before the lesson. It covers the VFR charts and ERSA, airspace and prohibited,
restricted and danger areas, last light and SARTIME, fuel requirements, dead reckoning
and the 1-in-60 rule, and radio calls at non-towered aerodromes. There's more reference
material than in earlier lessons, so it takes about 45–60 minutes, and your answers get
checked together at the start of the theory brief.

The pre-flight brief (~30 min, immediately before the flight) works best as an
interactive **whiteboard session**, not slides — checking the student's plan, drawing
the route as a strip map, and agreeing who flies and who navigates. These printable
notes give you the briefing components (aim, checking the plan, flight walk-through,
threats, airmanship emphasis on workload and lookout) with prompts and fill-in blanks,
plus a front page to sketch your own whiteboard plan and running order. Although it
will be helpful to plan and step through it yourself before the lesson, remember:
**modelling the process** of the pre-flight brief is more important than presenting a
polished pre-flight brief. This is why we do it interactively rather than a
presentation.
[Download the PDF of the briefing notes](/open-aviation-briefings/brief-slides/private-pilot-license/01-introduction-to-navigation/02-pre-flight-briefing-notes.pdf)
to print or use offline.

## Useful resources for students

- The NZ CAA Flight Instructor Guide's
  [Dead-reckoning navigation whiteboard](https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/licensing-and-certification/flight-instructor-guide/whiteboards/1.-Dead-reckoning-navigation.pdf)
  lays out the whole technique on one page.
- NZ CAA's [Practical Flying Guide 1: Visual Navigation](https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/publications/practical-flying-guides/practical-flying-guide-1-visual-navigation-v1.0.pdf)
  is an excellent, practical guide to structured visual navigation — including how to
  avoid "feature crawling" and keep your head up.
- The CASA [Visual Flight Rules Guide](https://www.casa.gov.au/resources-and-education/publications/industry-guides/pilot-guides-and-maps/visual-flight-rules-guide)
  is the Australian reference for everything in this lesson — charts, airspace, fuel,
  daylight, flight notification and non-controlled aerodrome procedures.
- CASA's [Out-n-Back](https://www.casa.gov.au/resources-and-education/education-and-training/out-n-back)
  video series follows real VFR navigation flights across Australia — the early
  episodes pair well with this lesson.
- The FAA [Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/phak),
  Chapter 16 (Navigation), covers pilotage, dead reckoning and the wind triangle in
  more depth.

## Example email to students before the lesson

If you are an instructor, feel free to modify this for your own use. Sending an email a few
days before a lesson is a great way to engage people in the learning before they even arrive
(if they have time and capacity).

> **Subject:** PPL Lesson 01 — Introduction to navigation
>
> Hi [name],
>
> This is the big one — your first cross-country flight, away from the training area
> and landing at another aerodrome. It's a longer day than usual: a planning session
> on the ground where you'll plan the actual route we'll fly, then the flight itself
> (about two and a half hours).
>
> Before the lesson, there's a **pre-lesson workbook** [1] to work through in your own
> time (about 45–60 minutes) — VFR charts and the ERSA, airspace, last light and
> SARTIME, fuel requirements, dead reckoning and the 1-in-60 rule, and radio calls at
> non-towered aerodromes. Bring your answers to the lesson. The live theory brief then
> builds on that (about two hours, in two parts, including planning the route
> together), followed by a 30-minute pre-flight brief.
>
> Please bring your navigation computer (or app), a pencil, and your charts if you
> have them.
>
> **When:** [day]
>
> See you then!
> [1] https://open-aviation-solutions.github.io/open-aviation-briefings/private-pilot-license/01-introduction-to-navigation/
