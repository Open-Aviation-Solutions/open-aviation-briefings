CASA Recreational Pilot License (Aeroplane) — CASA Sample Syllabus Lesson 24.

This lesson trains the response to deteriorating conditions away from the aerodrome —
weather, fuel, or growing unsure of your position — with the engine running normally
throughout: deciding early while still in full control, configuring the aeroplane,
flying a search-and-inspection pattern over a chosen field, the PAN call and passenger
brief, and completing a short-field landing. It also assesses the pre-flight
operational documents (MEL, NOTAMs, GNSS RAIM, ERSA, AIP), fuel management and
endurance calculations, and revises short-field take-off and landing from Lesson 22.

This lesson pairs its theory brief with a self-paced **pre-lesson workbook** — complete
it before the lesson. It covers why precautionary landings happen and why deciding
early matters, the turning illusion experienced flying low, fuel management and
endurance, the pre-flight operational documents, and the PAN call. It takes about
30–45 minutes, and your answers get checked together at the start of the pre-flight
brief.
[Download the PDF of the workbook](/open-aviation-briefings/brief-slides/recreational-pilot-license/24-precautionary-search-and-landing/00-pre-lesson-workbook.pdf)
to print or fill in on your device.

The pre-flight brief (~15 min, immediately before the flight) works best as an
interactive **whiteboard session**, not slides — stepping through the flight plan, the
inspection pattern, threats, and the "deciding early" judgement together with the
student. These printable notes give you the briefing components (aim, flight
walk-through, threats, airmanship emphasis) with prompts and fill-in blanks, plus a
front page to sketch your own whiteboard plan and running order. Although it will be
helpful to plan and step through it yourself before the lesson, remember: **modelling
the process** of the pre-flight brief is more important than presenting a polished
pre-flight brief. This is why we do it interactively rather than a presentation.
[Download the PDF of the briefing notes](/open-aviation-briefings/brief-slides/recreational-pilot-license/24-precautionary-search-and-landing/02-pre-flight-briefing-notes.pdf)
to print or use offline.

## Example email to students before the lesson

If you are an instructor, feel free to modify this for your own use. Sending an email a few
days before a lesson is a great way to engage people in the learning before they even arrive
(if they have time and capacity).

> **Subject:** Lesson 24 — Precautionary search and landing
>
> Hi [name],
>
> This lesson is a little different from Lesson 17 — the engine keeps running the
> whole time. It's about the judgement to decide early when weather, fuel, or your own
> position aren't going to plan, and the plan for what happens next: configuring the
> aeroplane, searching for and inspecting a field, and landing safely.
>
> Before the flight, there's a short **pre-lesson workbook** [1] to work through in your
> own time (about 30–45 minutes) — why precautionary landings happen, the turning
> illusion when flying low, fuel management and endurance, the pre-flight documents
> (MEL, NOTAMs, GNSS RAIM, ERSA, AIP), and the PAN call. Bring your answers to the
> lesson. The live theory brief then builds on that (about 20 minutes), focusing on the
> judgement, the search, and the flying — and we'll fly for about an hour, practising
> the sequence from varying positions, plus a short-field landing.
>
> **When:** [day]
>
> See you then!
> [1] https://open-aviation-solutions.github.io/open-aviation-briefings/recreational-pilot-license/24-precautionary-search-and-landing/
