CASA Recreational Pilot License (Aeroplane) — CASA Sample Syllabus Lesson 9.

This lesson trains the response to an engine problem close to the aerodrome, where height
and time are shortest: recognising an abnormal take-off and rejecting it, planning for an
engine failure with a pre-take-off safety brief, the engine failure after take-off flown
from memory and landing ahead, an engine failure elsewhere in the circuit, and the glide
approach and landing that ties them together. It is the last new circuit skill before the
pre-solo circuits of Lesson 10, and it also introduces engine start malfunctions (such as a
fire on start-up).

This lesson pairs its theory brief with a self-paced **pre-lesson workbook** — complete it
before the lesson. It covers the background/factual material: why engines fail and how the
pre-flight and run-up prevent it, abnormal take-offs and the rejected take-off, why not to
turn back after an engine failure after take-off, partial power after take-off, and the
factors that affect how far the aeroplane will glide — plus a preview video of a circuit
emergencies lesson. It takes about 30–45 minutes, and your answers get checked together at
the start of the pre-flight brief.
[Download the PDF of the workbook](/open-aviation-briefings/brief-slides/recreational-pilot-license/09-circuit-emergencies/00-pre-lesson-workbook.pdf)
to print or fill in on your device.

## Theory Brief

The live session builds on the workbook: recognising an abnormal take-off and the
rejected take-off, the pre-take-off safety brief, the engine failure after take-off actions
and why we land ahead, how the options change when the engine fails elsewhere in the
circuit, and flying, judging and correcting a glide approach. View the slides on this page
below or [open the

The pre-flight brief (~15–20 min, immediately before the flight) works best as an
interactive **whiteboard session**, not slides — stepping through the flight plan, the
safety brief and engine failure rehearsal, and threats together with the student. These
printable notes give you the briefing components (aim, flight walk-through, safety brief
and actions rehearsal, threats) with prompts and fill-in blanks, plus a front page to
sketch your own whiteboard plan and running order. Although it will be helpful to plan and
step through it yourself before the lesson, remember: **modelling the process** of the
pre-flight brief is more important than presenting a polished pre-flight brief. This is
why we do it interactively rather than a presentation.
[Download the PDF of the briefing notes](/open-aviation-briefings/brief-slides/recreational-pilot-license/09-circuit-emergencies/02-pre-flight-briefing-notes.pdf)
to print or use offline.

## Useful resources for students

- The NZ Civil Aviation Authority has an [engine failure after take-off whiteboard](https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/licensing-and-certification/flight-instructor-guide/whiteboards/fig-whiteboard-ct-efato.pdf)
  and a [glide approach whiteboard](https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/licensing-and-certification/flight-instructor-guide/whiteboards/fig-whiteboard-ct-ga.pdf),
  which pair well with this lesson's theory.
- The ATSB's [Avoidable Accidents No. 3 — Managing partial power loss after take-off in single-engine aircraft](https://www.atsb.gov.au/publications/avoidable-accidents/2022/aviation/avoidable-accidents-no-3-managing-partial-power-loss)
  is the best single read on what goes wrong after take-off, and why.
- The FAA [Airplane Flying Handbook, Chapter 18 (Emergency Procedures)](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/airplane_handbook)
  covers engine failure after take-off in more depth.

## Example email to students before the lesson

If you are an instructor, feel free to modify this for your own use. Sending an email a few
days before a lesson is a great way to engage people in the learning before they even arrive
(if they have time and capacity).

> **Subject:** Lesson 9 — Circuit emergencies
>
> Hi [name],
>
> This lesson is about what to do if the engine has a problem close to the aerodrome — on
> the take-off roll, just after take-off, or elsewhere in the circuit — and about gliding
> the aeroplane to a landing. It sounds more alarming than it is: every failure is
> simulated, the engine is fine throughout, and full power is always there to go around.
> It's also the last new circuit skill before you start working towards your first solo.
>
> Before the flight, there's a short **pre-lesson workbook** [1] to work through in your
> own time (about 30–45 minutes) — why engines fail and how we prevent it, the rejected
> take-off, why we don't turn back, partial power after take-off, and how far the
> aeroplane glides. Bring your answers to the lesson. The live theory brief then builds on
> that (about 35–40 minutes), and we'll fly for about an hour in the circuit, practising
> simulated engine failures and glide approaches.
>
> **When:** [day]
>
> See you then!
> [1] https://open-aviation-solutions.github.io/open-aviation-briefings/recreational-pilot-license/09-circuit-emergencies/
