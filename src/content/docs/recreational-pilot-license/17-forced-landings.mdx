CASA Recreational Pilot License (Aeroplane) — CASA Sample Syllabus Lesson 17.

This lesson trains the response to an engine failure away from the aerodrome: the
immediate/vital actions flown from memory, choosing and continually reassessing a
suitable field, flying and correcting the glide approach, and recognising an over- or
undershoot before it becomes unrecoverable. It also assesses engine start/shutdown
malfunctions and emergencies, and revises climbing and descending.

This lesson pairs its theory brief with a self-paced **pre-lesson workbook** — complete it
before the lesson. It covers the background/factual material: common causes of engine
failure, reading wind from the air, the field-selection criteria, the MAYDAY call,
detailed trouble checks, and passenger briefing/shutdown. It takes about 30–45 minutes, and
your answers get checked together at the start of the pre-flight brief.
[Download the PDF of the workbook](/open-aviation-briefings/brief-slides/recreational-pilot-license/17-forced-landings/00-pre-lesson-workbook.pdf)
to print or fill in on your device.

## Theory Brief

The live session builds on the workbook: the immediate/vital actions sequence, applying
your field-selection knowledge to a real scenario, planning and flying the approach
(including correcting if you're too high or too low), and recognising and managing an
over- or undershoot. View the slides on this page below or [open the
slides](/open-aviation-briefings/brief-slides/recreational-pilot-license/17-forced-landings/01-theory.html)

The pre-flight brief (~15–20 min, immediately before the flight) works best as an
interactive **whiteboard session**, not slides — stepping through the flight plan, the
immediate/vital actions rehearsal, and threats together with the student. These printable
notes give you the briefing components (aim, flight walk-through, immediate-actions
rehearsal, threats) with prompts and fill-in blanks, plus a front page to sketch your own
whiteboard plan and running order. Although it will be helpful to plan and step through it
yourself before the lesson, remember: **modelling the process** of the pre-flight brief is
more important than presenting a polished pre-flight brief. This is why we do it
interactively rather than a presentation.
[Download the PDF of the briefing notes](/open-aviation-briefings/brief-slides/recreational-pilot-license/17-forced-landings/02-pre-flight-briefing-notes.pdf)
to print or use offline.

## Example email to students before the lesson

If you are an instructor, feel free to modify this for your own use. Sending an email a few
days before a lesson is a great way to engage people in the learning before they even arrive
(if they have time and capacity).

> **Subject:** Lesson 17 — Forced landings
>
> Hi [name],
>
> This lesson trains one of the most important sequences in your whole training: what to do
> if the engine fails away from the aerodrome. Like stalling, it sounds more alarming than
> it is — every part of it is practised from a safe height, with full power always
> available to go around.
>
> Before the flight, there's a short **pre-lesson workbook** [1] to work through in your
> own time (about 30–45 minutes) — common causes of engine failure, reading wind from the
> air, choosing a field, the MAYDAY call, and the trouble-check/passenger-brief/shutdown
> actions. Bring your answers to the lesson. The live theory brief then builds on that
> (about 20–25 minutes), focusing on the sequence itself, flying the approach, and the
> judgement calls — and we'll fly for about an hour, practising simulated engine failures
> from varying heights and positions.
>
> **When:** [day]
>
> See you then!
> [1] https://open-aviation-solutions.github.io/open-aviation-briefings/recreational-pilot-license/17-forced-landings/
